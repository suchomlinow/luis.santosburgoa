About Me

I'm an M.S. candidate in Computational Analysis and Public Policy at the Harris School of Public Policy at the University of Chicago. I currently work as a Policy Research Fellow in the City of Chicago Mayor's Office, where I support research on municipal finance and tax policy. At the University of Chicago, I also teach coding at the Harris School of Public Policy and the Department of Computer Science.

Before coming to Chicago, I spent more than five years in the Directorate General of Economic Research at the Bank of Mexico, where I worked as a Specialty Leader and Information and Business Analyst. I hold a bachelor's degree in Industrial Engineering from ITAM in Mexico.

My work sits at the intersection of data, public policy, economics, and clear communication. I am especially interested in how strong public institutions and locally grounded solutions can improve people's lives.

What I'm Working On

  • Municipal finance and tax policy

    Evaluating municipal tax proposals for the City of Chicago to help address the city's fiscal deficit.

  • Entrepreneurship training

    Supporting a randomized controlled trial in the Dominican Republic with the World Bank that studies how entrepreneurship training affects participants' economic opportunities and outcomes.

  • Central bank communication

    Researching the communication channels and mechanisms used by the Bank of Mexico to make monetary policy and economic information clearer and more accessible.
